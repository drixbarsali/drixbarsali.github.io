Hello everyone! From time to time, I try to bring new texts to the table, recent discussions, and so on. This time, I want to talk directly to those who are beginning in the wonderful world of creating new things.

Listen, it’s not only about code. I know that you need to be prepared for an interview and, in fact, you will code every day for a long period of time. But it’s not only that. The most important aspects of evolving in your profession are not just IFs and ELSES. It’s about solving problems, understanding what is **under the hood**, being **creative** to keep it simple and stupid, adding new discussions to our **community**, and b**eing kind to yourself and others**. It’s not about trying to be the best but bringing everyone together to a higher level. It’s about **growing** together.

So following that, I will raise some points that I think are important to be a great developer and bring our profession to a higher level.

**Understand What You Are Doing**

One of the most important things in our profession is understanding what is really happening under the hood before starting coding. Whether it’s fixing a bug or adding a new feature, you need to spend some time understanding why.

> Why is this feature needed? What is really causing this bug?
> 

It’s not just about adding IFs and pushing to GitLab. It’s about taking your time and asking around to have a clear view of the real cause.

I sometimes say that this initial part of developing is like being some sort of Sherlock Holmes, following tracks to get the root cause. That kind of skill is not attached to how many algorithms you can create. It’s attached to how good you are at understanding the big picture, not only for the project but also for how the code really works.

Remember: Code is not a cake recipe. Following some steps won’t necessarily make you a cake. This is also important for your interview skills. Understand deeply what an interviewer is asking in your live coding session before moving on with your code.

**KISS Is Really Important**

Keep it simple and stupid (KISS). That is one of the concepts that I love the most. It goes against the principle that developers are rocket scientists.

You don’t need to write complicated code to achieve results.

Keeping it simple and stupid will not only help others at every level understand what your code is about but also help you with your unit tests.

You’re not only writing code for yourself but most of the time for a team. They will thank you after saving time reading your simple and stupid code.

Remember that a language is only a package of FORs, IFs, VARs, and so on. Being clear about what your methods are doing is really important.

**Sense of Community**

The internet brought everybody into the discussion and gave a voice to everyone. So let’s bring everyone into our discussion.

Being a developer is part of a huge worldwide community. I dream that one day we won’t have one huge ego guy screaming around on StackOverflow like he’s the owner of the truth. Sure we have more performant codes and sure we have cool ways to make things. But as I said before: **it’s not only about code; it’s also about people**.

It’s about being part of a huge **community** without worrying about your level. It’s about trying to bring more people inside and not making them leave. It’s about **learning every day** and **being kind to yourself and others**.

**In conclusion**: we’re not working only with code. Not that code isn’t important — **it is** — but also understands some of these topics.

I hope these topics find a way to help you grow because that’s what everyone is looking for: **growth as a person and as a professional**.
