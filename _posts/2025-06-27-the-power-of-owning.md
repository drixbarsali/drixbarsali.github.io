### The Power of Owning Your Mistakes

Throughout my long career in development, I’ve seen it all.

From starting as a junior developer to growing into a senior role - taking on more responsibilities and making critical decisions on client software - I’ve noticed one consistent pattern across every team I’ve worked with:

### No one takes ownership of mistakes

It’s always someone else’s fault.

And then, most people lack the courage to admit when they don't know something - when there is more to learn.

At the core of this issue? **Ego**. 

That needs to be perfect all the time.

### The Illusion of Perfection

Perfection is a myth. No one knows everything, and no one gets everything right on the first attempt. And yet, in most teams, there is this implicit pressure to always have the answers, to never be vulnerable, and to never own up to fault.

This attitude is toxic. It stifles progress, results in meaningless blame-shifting, and cultivates a culture in which people are more interested in protecting their image than in solving problems.

But here's the truth: Mistakes are unavoidable.

The difference between an excellent professional and an average one isn't the lack of mistakes - it's how they respond to them.

### Why Taking Ownership of Mistakes is Important

**1. Quicker Problem-Solving**

When mistakes are denied or blamed on other people, they get resolved more slowly. Taking ownership of a problem lets the team concentrate on solutions instead of spending time accusing each other.

**2. Deeper Team Trust**

An accountable team is a productive team. When people feel safe taking responsibility for their mistakes, collaboration improves, and innovation thrives.

**3. Personal and Professional Growth**

Growth comes from learning, and learning comes from failure. The best professionals aren't the ones who never fail—they're the ones who fail, learn, and adapt quickly.

**4. Better Leadership**

Leaders who take responsibility for their mistakes send a powerful message. They create a culture in which improvement and honesty matter more than ego and blame.

### The Hardest Part: Owning the Bad

It's easy to take credit for the good you do on a project or team. The difficult thing is to own up to the bad. But here's the truth: The sooner you handle the things you don't want to handle -first with yourself, then with others - the sooner you'll **grow**. And growth is everything.

Owning your mistakes doesn't just make you a better professional - it makes you a better person. When you set an example, others feel comfortable doing the same. They realize that mistakes aren't failures; they're stepping on the road to growth.

### Mistakes Are Fixable - If You Own Them

There are only two things in life with no solution: Death and taxes.

Everything else? The moment you acknowledge the problem, you’re already on your way to fixing it.
