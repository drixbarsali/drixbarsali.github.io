Today I want to talk about politics. To do so, I have to take a step back and break away from the meaning we are used to.

I don't want to talk about power dynamics or talk show theatricality. I am talking about politics in its noblest and original sense: that uniquely human capacity to govern relationships and transform conflict into collaboration. In other words, politics understood as **POLIS**.

In my work, I constantly move between two worlds: technology and the psychological safety of teams. And I've realized that politics, when understood the right way, is a true relational operating system.

Because when it is genuine, it becomes an ethical exchange. It is no longer manipulation; it is simply strategic empathy. It means understanding that the other person has different needs from mine and engineering a solution where both gain something. It is a perfect hybrid between hard and soft skills: it requires the technical ability to analyze the data on the table combined with the human sensitivity needed to create win-win solutions, even where there seems to be room for only one winner.

### How does this skill manifest in a team's daily routine?

It all starts with **Ego Management**. To see the big picture, you must step back, stop wanting to be right at all costs, and start governing a collective process.

Linked to this is a fundamental pairing: **Courage and Kindness**. We must overturn the idea that kindness is weakness. On the contrary, kindness is a strong method for managing conflict without destroying your counterpart. But it requires enormous courage: the courage to doubt your own certainties and to embrace someone else's truth without feeling defeated.

Next comes **Active Listening** paired with the **Ecology of Words**. Often in teams, people listen only to reply. Good politics requires listening to understand. This must be accompanied by care for language: using precise, transparent, and non-violent words. Clean words build bridges, while aggressive language only raises cortisol levels in the team.

This leads us to **Transparent Negotiation**, where there are no hidden agendas. The formula is simple: *"I need X, you need Y: how do we build Z together?"*. From this perspective, compromise is not a sellout—it is a noble synthesis. It is true care for the common good: if the exchange is fair, the team grows stronger. If the exchange is toxic or predatory, sooner or later the system crashes.

Practicing politics as a soft skill does not mean always agreeing or avoiding confrontation. On the contrary: it means having the courage to say no with extreme kindness, bringing valuable alternatives to the table instead of merely blocking the process.

This is the true glue of psychological safety.

When people know that their needs will be heard, analyzed, and never weaponized against them, collaboration stops being a contractual effort. It becomes the natural way of working.

We need to reclaim the deep meaning of this word. Doing politics means having the courage to be fiercely kind while designing the future, ensuring that every interaction leaves people richer than when they started.

How do you manage conflict in your teams? Have you ever thought of politics in this light?
