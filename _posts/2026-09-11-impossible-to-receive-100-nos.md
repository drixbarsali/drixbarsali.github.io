Today I am not talking about team health, but I challenge you — whatever goal you are pursuing:

A JOB?  
A SALE?  
A PROJECT?  
AN IDEA?

... I challenge you to receive 100 NOs.

This is the provocation I made to the FELLOW I was mentoring. I have been, and hope to continue being, a mentor (for the upcoming BOOSTs) for Sistech. My "fellow" is a refugee woman who is trying to change her life; a person who, after experiencing the worst, had the courage to start over, learning a new language and a new culture, far from her relatives and the country to which she had dedicated part of her life.

Courage. Many do not understand that you are not born with courage: you acquire it. Life sometimes beats you down to the point that the only thing you have left is that organ pumping blood. And you only remember to breathe. For some, the cruelty of life pushes towards two paths: letting go and giving up, or moving forward. That moving forward is what we call courage.

It is nothing more than small decisions to be made in a universe that, for some, is 0 or 1. Here it is: these people were forced to leave their lives behind, they didn't give up, but now the challenge is another: finding a role in the development world.

And it is on this that I challenged her to get 100 NOs. In her case, I lowered the stakes to "10"; in my opinion, they were enough. Maybe it sounds crazy, but I challenge you, dear reader: 100 business proposals, 100 CVs sent... whatever it is, I challenge you to receive 100 NOs.

I too, when I decided to drop everything, carried this challenge in my pocket! Of course, it is not comparable to a "fellow" who had to change everything not by choice, but the challenge made me laugh more than cry. I wanted to try and prove that the rule of failure is nonsense.

With every NO I received, I would say to myself: "only 99, 98, 97 to go...". In fact, I found my first job here in Italy and I still had a good 84 NOs left over!

### Why the "100 NOs" Framework Works in Tech Careers

When you are searching for your first developer role, switching countries, or pitching a technical architecture to stakeholders, rejection feels deeply personal. After three or four rejections—or worse, automated ghosting emails—most candidates stop applying or start doubting their entire skill set.

The **100 NOs Rule** flips the psychological reward system inside your brain:
1. **It decouples outcome from effort**: You cannot control whether a specific company has a hiring freeze or picks an internal candidate. You *can* control how many meaningful applications, portfolio projects, and technical conversations you initiate.
2. **Every "NO" becomes progress**: When your explicit goal is to collect 100 rejections, a "NO" is no longer a failure—it is a step closer to completing the challenge. The fear of rejection loses its paralyzing power.
3. **Iterative feedback loops MAKE it impossible to reach 100**: In software engineering, we never deploy version 1.0 and stop iterating. If you actively reflect after every 5–10 interviews—refining how you explain your code, improving your GitHub READMEs, sharpening your answers on state management or testing—your conversion rate compounds so quickly that you will land a "YES" long before you ever hit 100.

### How to Turn Rejections into Career Momentum: A Practical Playbook

During my mentoring sessions with **Sistech**, we broke down the job search into actionable engineering sprints rather than passive CV drops:

- **Treat every interview as a system diagnostic**: After a technical interview, write down the exact questions that made you hesitate. Whether it was RxJS operators, JavaScript closures, or system design, turn that gap into a 48-hour study topic. By your fifth interview, the questions that once tripped you up become your strongest talking points.
- **Show, don't just list**: A resume lists keywords; a clean, well-documented repository or a short technical write-up proves how you think. Even a small project with unit tests, clear commit history, and a thoughtful architectural explanation sets you apart from 90% of applicants.
- **Build human connections, not just application numbers**: Reach out to developers and Tech Leads in local tech communities, attend meetups, and ask genuine questions about how their teams work. People remember authenticity and determination far more than a generic cover letter.
