### About the Session: What Can Pirates Teach Software Teams?

#### Yohooo! – The Art of Being Less Developer and More Pirate (Even with Less Rum and More Coffee) 🏴‍☠️

What can 18th-century pirates teach us about teamwork, leadership, and engineering culture in modern software development? Much more than Hollywood clichés suggest.

In this session hosted by **GDG Brescia**, Adriano Barsali takes us beyond cinematic stereotypes to explore a lesser-known historical reality: pirate crews were remarkably advanced, self-organized communities built on voluntary collaboration, shared written rules, mutual accountability, and collective support.

Starting from their historical **"Pirate Code" (*Articles of Agreement*)**, we tackle one of the toughest obstacles facing software engineering teams today: **ego**.

Through real-world engineering stories, practical team scenarios, and excursions into the history of piracy, we explore four core pillars for building healthier, more effective development teams:

#### 1. ⚓ Psychological Safety in Engineering Teams
How do we build an environment where asking questions, admitting a mistake, or requesting help early is never perceived as a sign of weakness?
- Why developers stay silent when blocked—and the hidden cost of that silence on sprint velocity and mental health.
- How Tech Leads and Senior Engineers can model vulnerability during standups, architecture discussions, and blameless post-mortems.

#### 2. ⚓ Junior & Senior Dynamics: Breaking the Double Myth
In many teams, two invisible walls slow down learning:
- **Junior developers** fear making mistakes or asking "obvious" questions during code reviews.
- **Senior developers** feel pressured by the unrealistic expectation that they must always have the right answer on the spot.
When we replace hierarchy with horizontal pairing and shared curiosity, juniors grow faster and seniors are freed from the burden of perfection.

#### 3. ⚓ True Collaboration vs. Isolated Silos
How do we transition from a group of individual developers working on isolated tickets to a cohesive crew that shares responsibility, domain knowledge, and goals?
- Using the pirate separation of powers (Captain vs. Quartermaster) to balance rapid technical decision-making with team-wide consensus.
- Eliminating knowledge silos and reducing the team's "bus factor" through empathetic Pull Request reviews and living documentation.

#### 4. ⚓ Taming the Ego
Why a software team's real value is never measured by a single developer's heroic commit, but by the crew's collective ability to move forward together.
- Writing simple, readable, maintainable code for the team rather than "clever" code for one's own ego.
- Recognizing mentorship, unblocking teammates, and constructive code reviews as first-class engineering contributions.
